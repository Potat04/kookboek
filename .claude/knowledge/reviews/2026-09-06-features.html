What Kookboek is missing

Six imagined users walked through the app. I read what they wanted, checked it against the code, threw out what was wrong or off-vision, and ranked the rest.

Judged against the vision Anton gave today: a paper-like, calm recipe book for himself, family and friends. Cookbook first, kitchen tools later. No services except the sites recipes come from and possibly a backup. Dutch and English.

The one-paragraph verdict

The app does its core job well. Saving a page and reading it later is solid, honest about failure, and pleasant to look at. What it lacks is everything around that moment. The data lives in one place and nowhere else. A recipe cannot leave the phone except as a link back to the site. The library is a flat list that stops working somewhere around forty recipes. And the screen you actually cook from is a scrolling document, not a page you can read from the far side of the counter. None of the big gaps require breaking the vision. Most of them are what a paper cookbook already does, moved to a phone.

If I had to pick an order
  1. Backup file you own. Everything else assumes the recipes survive. Today they exist in one SQLite file and an Android backup nobody can inspect.
  2. Two small ones with big payoff. Remember the servings someone cooked for, and remember the sort order. Both are one column or one preference.
  3. Cook mode, with timers handed to the phone's clock. This is where the app is used, and today it is the weakest screen for that.
  4. Sections of the book. Labels the reader controls, fed by the parser's keywords. The library needs this before it reaches a hundred recipes.
  5. Hand a recipe to someone. The audience is family and friends. A cookbook gets passed round the table.
  6. Own photos, then print, then recipes that arrive as text, then the tablet layout.

Major

Each of these is a new capability, touches more than one layer, and changes what the app is for someone. All seven fit the vision. I checked each against the "what is deliberately not there" list in the architecture notes, and none of them add a server, an account, a DI container or a domain layer.

1. A backup file you own

majorfitsasked by D, O, F, T

One zip with a recipes JSON and the images folder, written wherever the reader points the system file picker. Restore reads the same file back and merges by id. That picker already offers Google Drive, Nextcloud, a USB stick, whatever is installed, so there is no SDK and no account code. Anton mentioned account-based backups as open to suggestion. I would not build one. The file picker gives the same result for free and keeps the "no services" line intact.

This is the one I would build first. A recipe book that lives in one SQLite file is a recipe book with a single point of failure, and the README's own data page says this is "precisely the data that exists nowhere else".

Touches RecipeRepository, ImageStore, a new data/Backup.kt, a settings row, ACTION_CREATE_DOCUMENT and ACTION_OPEN_DOCUMENT. No schema change. The JSON shape becomes a public format, so version it from day one.

2. Hand a recipe to someone

majorfitsasked by F, D, O

Today the only way out is the link to the original site. Two exits are missing. First, plain text through the share sheet, so a recipe lands in WhatsApp readable. Second, a single-recipe file with its photo that another phone's Kookboek opens straight into the library. The file gets its own extension and mime type and is opened with a view intent, not a send intent. That keeps the rule from the README that Kookboek stays out of every other app's share menu.

The audience is family and friends. If two people in one household both run Kookboek and cannot pass a recipe between them, the app is missing its most obvious social moment.

Touches the manifest, ShareActivity or a sibling for incoming files, RecipeRepository.importFile, the overflow menu on RecipeScreen, and the selection bar (share several at once). Shares the JSON shape with the backup.

3. Sections of the book

majorfitsasked by K, F, O, T

Labels the reader creates and assigns, one recipe in several, shown as a chip row under the search field and settable from selection mode. The parser already reads keywords, category and cuisine into tags and shows two of them on the detail screen. Those become suggestions when you label, not a second concept beside labels. One idea, not "tags and collections".

This does break a rule the UI notes state on purpose, that selection mode offers exactly the two actions a single recipe has. Labelling would be a third. I think the rule is right in spirit and this is the exception worth making, because assigning ten recipes to "Weeknight" one by one is the tedium that stops people organizing at all.

Every paper cookbook has tabs. A flat list of three hundred cards with a search box is a search engine, not a book.

New labels and recipe_labels tables, migration to version 2, DAO joins, filter in KookboekViewModel, chips in LibraryScreen, a picker sheet. The existing tags JSON column stays as the parser's raw input.

4. Cook mode

majorfitsasked by C, O, T

A second way to read the same recipe. One step per screen in the largest serif the palette allows, tap the right half for next and the left half for back, ingredients as a sheet you pull up from the bottom without leaving the step. Keep-screen-on already exists. The step counter is the only chrome. The weeknight cook's whole report was about scrolling past the photo and description to get back to the salt, then losing the step.

The moment the phone is on the counter is the moment the app exists for, and today that screen is a long document designed for reading on the sofa. Both readings are right. They should be two modes.

New ui/CookScreen.kt, a fifth navigation destination, an entry button under the method header. Reuses Scaling and the check state. Pairs with the timer item below.

5. Your own photos

majorfitsasked by F, O, T, and the README's own not-yet list

Three cases share one mechanism. A handwritten recipe gets a photo of the dish. A fetched recipe whose picture turned out to be the site's logo gets replaced. And grandma's card gets photographed and kept as an attachment beside the typed-out version, because the handwriting is part of the recipe. The photo picker needs no permission on current Android. The picked image goes through the same resize and JPEG path that fetched images do.

Fits without reservation. The family persona put it plainly: the heirloom recipes are the ones that need this most, and they are exactly the ones the browser share cannot bring in.

ImageStore.saveFromUri, a photo row in EditScreen, PickVisualMedia. The attachment case adds an attachmentFile column and a migration; the first two cases do not.

6. Recipes that arrive as text, not links

majorfitsasked by F, and me

Half the recipes in a family travel as a WhatsApp message, a note, or a pasted block from a PDF. The app already accepts shared text, then answers "There was no link in that" and stops. Instead, text without a URL should open the editor pre-filled, with a heuristic split: the lines that look like quantities become ingredients, the paragraphs after them become steps, the first short line is the title. The reader fixes what the split got wrong before saving, so the heuristic can be modest.

Same share gesture, same mime type, same promise. It only widens what "share to Kookboek" means from "a page" to "a recipe, wherever it was".

ShareActivity branches on "no URL found", new pure parse/TextRecipe.kt with tests against real pasted messages as fixtures, EditScreen gains a prefill path.

7. The book opened flat: tablet and landscape

majorfitsasked by me; F implied it

A tablet on the counter is how many households cook from a screen, and a phone turned sideways is how one person does it with the phone propped against the tiles. Both get the same layout: ingredients on the left page, method on the right, photo and title above. The library becomes two or three columns of cards at width. This is the one item where the paper metaphor gives the design for free, because a cookbook lies open on two pages.

Major because every screen is affected, not because any one part is hard. Cook mode and this should be designed together so the step view knows what to do with a second column.

Window size classes in MainActivity, a two-pane branch in RecipeScreen, a grid branch in LibraryScreen. The palettes, typography and paper grain need nothing.

Medium

Each of these is a focused change with a clear edge. Several are the small end of the majors above and could be built first as a stepping stone.

Remember the servings you cooked for

mediumfitsC, D, O

The stepper resets to the page's number every time the recipe opens. Someone who always cooks for six sets it to six every time. Store the chosen number per recipe, keep "scaled from 4" visible, and let the editor's servings field stay the original. Everyone who cooked from the app in their head asked for this.

One column, one migration, RecipeScreen reads it instead of local state.

Ingredients within reach while in the method

mediumfitsC, O

Even without cook mode, the normal screen needs a way back to the salt without scrolling past the photo. A "show ingredients" pull-up from the method section, or the ingredients header pinning to the top as it scrolls off. The list is already scaled and ticked, so this is presentation only.

RecipeScreen only. A sticky header in LazyColumn or a small ModalBottomSheet.

Print, or save as PDF

mediumfitsO, F, T

The older reader sticks recipes to the fridge. Android's print manager takes an HTML document and offers every printer and "Save as PDF". Rendering the recipe as a one-page HTML in the palette's ink on white is a small template. This is also the honest route for "send as PDF" without adding a PDF library.

An app whose whole identity is paper should be able to become paper.

A PrintRecipe.kt that builds HTML from a Recipe and a WebView print adapter. One menu item.

Honest, specific failure

mediumfitsF, D, O

"That didn't work" is honest but not useful. The repository already knows whether the site blocked the app, the page had no recipe markup, the network was down, or the check timed out. Each of those has one right next step and should say it. After a batch refetch, the selection should stay on the ones that failed instead of clearing, so "3 failed" is something you can act on.

"Never lie about what worked" is already a rule in the UI notes. This finishes it by also saying why.

FailureReason grows a case or two, strings in both files, ShareSheet and the toasts, refreshSelected keeps the failed ids.

"Help Kookboek read this site"

mediumfitsme, D

On a link-only or partly-read recipe, a button that saves the fetched HTML to a file the reader can send to Anton. That file is a fixture, and the testing rules say new site support starts with a fixture. With family running the app, this turns every failed import into a test case without anyone having to explain how to save a web page.

Cheap, on-vision, and it feeds the one kind of work the kitchen-tool persona said is worth doing right now, which is parser reach.

RecipeRepository keeps the last fetched HTML for a recipe (in memory or a temp file), one button in CouldNotRead, a share of a .html file.

Made it

mediumfits if quietK, T, D

One tap that stamps today's date on the recipe, with an optional line that lands in the notes. Then "last cooked" as a sort, and "never cooked" as a filter. No stars, no counts on the card. The collector's real question was "which of these three hundred have I actually made", and a date answers that.

This is the pencil tick in the margin of a real cookbook. The moment it becomes a rating or a streak it stops being that.

A lastCookedAt column and migration, a menu item, a sort option, a filter chip.

Filters that stack

mediumfitsK, F

Favourites plus a label plus "under 30 minutes" plus a site, all as chips in the same row the favourites chip lives in now. Not a filter screen. The ViewModel already composes filters in one combine, so this is mostly adding flows and chips.

KookboekViewModel.visible, FilterRow. Depends on labels for the label chip; the time and site chips can land first.

Refetch shows what it will replace

mediumfitsD, F

Refetch keeps notes, favourite and ticks, which two personas wrongly feared it did not. What it does replace is the ingredients and steps, including any typo the reader fixed by hand. Remember whether a recipe was hand-edited and, if so, say so before refetching, or show the two ingredient lists side by side and let the reader choose.

An editedAt column, a confirm step in refresh, a small diff view.

Ingredient groups

mediumfitsC, T

Steps already carry a section ("Voor de saus"). Ingredients do not, so a layered recipe shows one long list while the method is neatly grouped. WP Recipe Maker and Tasty both publish ingredient group headings, and the editor already has "# heading" syntax for steps that can apply to ingredients too.

Parser plugin path plus a fixture that has groups, a section column on ingredients and a migration, the same heading rendering as steps.

Timers handed to the phone's clock

mediumfitsC, T, me

When a step says "bak 25 minuten" or "simmer for 20 min", underline the duration. Tapping it fires the system set-timer intent with the recipe title as the label. The clock app does the ringing, the app builds no timer UI and holds no background service. Two languages, one regex, tested against the fixtures.

The most on-brand kitchen helper there is. It gives the phone's own clock the job instead of growing one.

A pure parse/Durations.kt, an annotated string in StepRow, AlarmClock.ACTION_SET_TIMER.

Rejected, and why

Things a persona asked for that I would not build. Each one either breaks a line the vision draws on purpose, or undoes a decision the code already made for a reason written down next to it.

  • Any sync server or account. The backup file through the system picker already reaches Google Drive, Nextcloud and a USB stick. An account would add a dependency to get the same result.
  • Cloud or model-based parsing. Anton ruled it out, and the fixture-driven parser is the reason the app can promise offline and private.
  • Notes or site favicons on the library card. The card is at its width limit already; the UI notes record that even a slightly larger meta line truncated "15 stuks". More on the card means less on the card.
  • Boolean or fuzzy search syntax. Accent-insensitive matching fixes the real misses. "spicy NOT thai" is for a search engine.
  • Per-palette darkness sliders. The six palettes are hand-checked against ten contrast pairs in both schemes. A slider throws that away for a preference almost nobody sets.
  • An onboarding carousel. The empty state already teaches the one gesture that matters. One hint for long-press is the whole tutorial.
  • Dynamic colour. Already decided against in the UI notes, for the right reason. The paper is the identity.
  • A dashboard home screen with "recently cooked", "suggested for tonight", "your stats". The library is the home screen. It is a book, not a feed.

Where the personas were wrong

Haiku agents reading code make mistakes, and passing those on would waste Anton's time. These claims did not survive a look at the code.

  • "No duplicate detection." There is. The repository normalizes the URL and returns AlreadySaved; the share sheet says "Already in there".
  • "The byline shows 24Kitchen twice." RecipeScreen already dedupes author and site case-insensitively.
  • "Refetch throws away my notes." It keeps notes, favourite and ticks explicitly. It does replace hand-edited ingredients and steps, which is the real, smaller issue listed under medium.
  • "Tags are dead data." They are searched and two of them are shown on the detail screen. What is true is that they cannot be filtered on or edited.
  • "No way to search by time." Sorting by quickest exists. A filter chip does not, and that is the small item above.
  • "The stepper buttons are 18dp." The icon is. The touch target is the standard 48dp. The fix is visual, which is why it sits under quality of life.

Two things I would watch

First, the labels feature and the shopping list both want to break the "two actions in selection mode" rule. Break it once, for labels, and hold the line after that. Second, three of the medium items add a column each. Batch them into one migration to version 2 rather than shipping versions 2, 3 and 4 in a month. The schema is checked in for exactly this reason.
